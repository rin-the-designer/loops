Research Background

Building on my previous studio project, where I conducted a drawing-as-elicitation workshop, I wanted to revisit the method with refinements based on my reflections. Whereas the earlier study was carried out in a workshop format with multiple participants drawing together in the same space, this iteration was designed to explore whether similar patterns would emerge in an isolated setting, where each participant worked individually.

In the earlier study, participants were asked to draw both shapes(or a group of shapes) and objects in response to the sequence of death, life, and loop within the shared space. This approach, however, did not yield meaningful differences between shape based and object based sketches. For this study, I narrowed the focus to a single prompt, a pattern, in order to examine both commonalities across participants and the traces of individual personality.

Specifically, participants were presented with four words, absurdity, meaningful, error, and success, along with their dictionary definitions. They were then asked to intuitively draw patterns that came to mind(like the previous study). By having each participant work in isolation, the study excludes the possibility of repetition or influence that might arise in a collective setting.

Research Plan

Step 1. Drawing Prompt

Participants are asked to draw sketches (the prompt was modified during first iteration, from sketches to patterns) patterns of multiple concepts. Participants are given words in a specific order alongside their dictionary definition.

  • Absurdity
    • The quality or state of being ridiculous or wildly unreasonable
  • Meaningful
    • Having meaning / having a serious, important, or useful quality or purpose.
  • Error
    • A mistake / the state or condition of being wrong in conduct or judgment.
  • Success
    • The accomplishment of an aim or purpose.

Step 2. Drawing

Participants will be asked to draw and object or a pattern of what they think of the given words.

Step 3. Asking intentions

After all 4 sketches are complete, the participants are asked about their intentions in the patterns, while taking a look at them together.

Findings

It was interesting to see the results, especially the first prompt “Absurd”. I found it absurd in the way that the patterns that the participants drew revealed a particular pattern, but having different thoughts in intentions. Despite being given identical dictionary definitions, participants understood the prompt similarly but developed distinctly different interpretations of the concept.

Future improvements

I'm curious how the patterns would differ if participants were given alternative dictionary definitions. For my first iteration, I provided a specific definition to ensure all participants started from the same foundational understanding.
