# Loops Presentation Script

## Introduction

Hi, I’m Rin Kim, designer from Korea, with an interest in how people interact with technology. During undergraduate I studied visual design and French. Although I focused more on design and forgot a lot of French, the project I'm presenting today started from my French background.

## Motivation

In my French courses back in undergrad, I was told to read The Myth of Sisyphus and The Stranger by Albert Camus in the original French text. It felt like so much work at first, but as I read through, I felt something... which is still hard to explain but some sort of connection with Sisyphus and Meursault. Although I was so involved with these text, they slipped out of my memory as I leaned towards design. It wasn't until last semester that they came back to me, as I was feeling empty, finding myself in involuntary loops.

## Motivation

Just like Sisyphus, I found myself in a loop of repetition. It wasn’t a loop filled with everything I wanted to do, it was more of a loop filled with everything I had to do. I was putting full effort in one assignment, then another. I woke up everyday to go to school, share my work and get more work. With also struggling to adjust to a new environment, I felt empty and a lot of things felt meaningless. That’s when I went back to The Myth of Sisyphus and The Stranger. Just by realizing the fact that I felt stuck made me confront it and embrace it. It felt less as a burden but more as something that I’m willing to partake.

## Definition

That lead me to define this concept of absurdity and agency in my own words. For me loops are continuous cycle of activities and circumstances that we were compelled to engage in or adhere to. And it is the thresholds that give me the autonomy to determine our actions and interpretations within these circumstances. After confronting what I was going through and defining my own terms for it, I decided to build a project out of it.

## Medium

Even when I felt stuck in my own loops, I was drawn to one thing: creative coding. Learning JavaScript, I realized that so much of what I was making literally ran on loops / a for loop just repeating an action over and over. That connection between repetition in my life and repetition in code is what made me choose code as my main medium for exploring loops and thresholds.

## Precedents

I looked for precedents in coded artworks and found two artists who create fully code-based pieces, Rafaël Rozendaal and Zach Lieberman. I was even able to meet Rafaël in an artist talk and ask him about his work. Their projects showed me how simple shapes and generative patterns can form powerful visual loops.

But in most of these works, the viewer is still outside the loop, just watching it run. I became more interested in what happens when the viewer themselves becomes part of the loop, when the code reacts to their body, their movement, or their attention.

## Interactivity

I realized the biggest difference between code-based work and something rendered in After Effects was interactivity. As a designer, that’s what pulled me towards coding / I could make the work react to a viewer’s trigger instead of staying fixed on a timeline. By using code, I wanted the artwork to respond to people’s presence and actions, and, just as I was learning to embrace my loops, I wanted viewers to have a chance to notice and reflect on their own loops and thresholds.

## Research Question

Which leads to my research question, "What narratives emerge from the tension between involuntary loops and chosen thresholds.

## Project

Loops is a collection of digital artworks on involuntary loops and chosen thresholds.

## Loop 1

Because this project started with Camus text, the first loop is a visual representation of the myth of Sisyphus.

Viewers can either drag the boulder with their mouse, or use their hand to push the boulder up the hill. I added a feature to make the boulder feel heavier as the audience pushes the circle more and more.

People showed different levels of engagement, some gave up quickly, some kept going on and on for minutes. And there was also someone deciding their own way to interact by keeping the boulder in the middle of the hill. I initially wanted to have people interact with it as long as possible, but I realized that leaving on their own choice is also a part of this work.

## Loop 2

Next is the Doors. I thought doors could have many different functions and meanings. It is one of the most ordinary object we can find yet every doorway marks a small shift in role and identity. In this project the viewers are confronted with endless black and white passage of doors. The repetition and colors mean the cycle and the duality of existence, life and death, light and dark, beginning and ending.

For this project, camera interaction is optional to control the viewing perspective of the passage.

One person got frustrated because of the slow speed, but then started to reflect how they are used to fast paced media and how they fast forwarded and skipped through content. Another interesting finding was that some people walked in place because they thought it triggered the animation, which I was only able to find while using a bigger screen for testing.

## Loop 3

Next project accumulation is a digital representation of a physical process of drawing black circles on a canvas, covering it with white, drawing circles and covering over and over. It was a project I did to find meaning in apparently meaningless process. After painting the canvas I found that even though from afar it still looks like a blank canvas, it had traces, different width, and it was much heavier than when I bought it because of the weight of the paint. On the digital version, it shows how lines of code is injected to accumulate in the Document Object Model, or the DOM.

Viewers can click on the screen to change the diameter of the circle and swap the colors.

Although this is one of my favorites, the meaning wasn't quite direct as the other projects since people needed knowledge on coding.

## Loop 5

Incense is a digital representation of a burning incense stick as a connection between life and death. In many of the asian culture, incense sticks are use for ceremonies and rituals, connecting the spirits of the living and the deceased. I've seen my family saying our ancestors have arrived by seeing the smoke drift by wind. And because it also changes direction when we move by, I thought it was a mutual interaction between us and those who have passed away.

For this project the smoke changes directions based on the position of the viewer, just as it would change direction based on the wind of people moving by.

## Loop 7

This project comes from an observation from a drawing activity where I asked people to draw patterns of “Absurdity” and “Meaningfulness”. People drew “Absurdity in randomized doodles, and “Meaningfulness” in a more constructed repetition. However, for me, the randomness felt like it had more freedom as if it was trying to explore the given canvas. Thus, I visualized the pattern and flipped its titles.

During testing, I was able to see people focusing more on the randomized pattern, just trying to see where the circle goes next. And they started discussing which they felt was more meaningful or meaningless, which was the kind of reflection that I wanted to see through this collection.

## Goals

The goal of this project is to invite viewers to reflect on their own loops and thresholds. The project suggests that rather than resisting these loops, we can accept their presence, embrace their absurdity, and ultimately regain a sense of agency.

Through multiple rounds of testing this semester, I was able to decide how I want to define this project. I want each project to have no beginning, no progress, and no ending so that the viewers can engage and leave on their own terms. Although I design these with a particular interaction in mind, I don't want to force them to interact the way I intended them to. I want them to explore and reflect the way they want to.
